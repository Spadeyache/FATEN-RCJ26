Parts & Fabrication
RCJ RescueLine / Off-design tasks

Outside the design work, two fabrication tasks gate the build: validating the new PCB and casting the new tires from a fresh mold. The PCB is a make-and-check item; the tire carries a real design objective (grip budget + front/rear balance), spelled out below.

Production tasks
01 · PCB
PCB test
Bench-verify the new board

Why The upgrades (in-wheel servo wiring, ToF, arm) ride on a revised board — it must be proven on the bench before assembly.

Do Check power rails, the servo bus and every sensor header (ToF, IMU, camera), then confirm a clean motor + sensor bring-up.

02 · TIRE MOLD
Tire mold creation
Same size · dual-zone tread · all four identical
Objective A softer, higher-grip tire that enlarges the grip budget (slopes & bumps) and removes the front/rear grip imbalance — while still scrubbing freely enough for skid-steer turning.

Why Our bench tests showed the robot's behaviour is set by grip, not geometry: the rotation axis is held by whichever tires grip, and on a slope the nose lifts when a turn scrubs through the grip budget and gravity drags the lighter end downhill. So the tire is the real lever — and it must do two opposite jobs at once: bite on bumps (off-road) yet still slip freely so a 4-wheel skid-steer can turn.

Do — dual-zone tread Cast a tire with a smooth centre band (clean line contact, easy turning, soft high-grip compound that holds on slopes) and low knobs only at the shoulders to catch bumps that come in from the side. Keep the size (gearing/×1.5 stay valid), and make all four identical so front/rear grip is balanced and the axis becomes predictable & software-controllable.

Watch Shoulder knobs must sit at or just below the centre diameter. If they stick out past it, the robot rides on the knobs on flat ground → noisy contact and unstable line following. Centre = primary contact; shoulders engage only on bumps.
